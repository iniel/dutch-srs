## 7. Conclusions and proposals

### Stages that found nothing or only cosmetics

1. **Re-review** (18 runs): 0 must_fix, 4 should_fix, all gloss hygiene after a mid-run `exceptions.json` change. (§4)
2. **Comprehension questions**: 178/178 answerable, zero discrimination. (§5)
3. **Grammar flags at `review`/`ignored` level**: 0/94 confirmed; R-PARTICIPLE 0/48. (§3)
4. **`nl-cohesion` C-TENSE / C-TERM**: 0/13 confirmed. (§6)
5. **Second fix round**: never used (0/9 rounds needed a second). (§1.1)

### Stages that must stay

1. **Language reviewer**: 20 of 22 must_fix, including the only ones with a reader-visible effect (sense-level vocabulary, dangling referents, un-Dutch constructions) and 2 of the 4 real restricted constructions (parser recall 2/4). (§1.2–1.3, §3)
2. **nl-check vocabulary layer**: it saw 12 of 22 must_fix lemmas (6 flagged, 6 passed-as-glossed), and its known-share / density gate is what keeps the translator inside the profile in the first place (V2 scenes: 1 must_fix in 7 vs V1's 9). It is cheap; the problem is only what it does with what it sees.
3. **Translator fix round** (one): 100 % of must_fix resolved in one round, 9/9.
4. **Cohesion editor as a memory step**, not as a text-editing step. (§6)

### Concrete cuts, with the evidence line

1. **Drop the re-review when v2 `nl-check` is clean and the fix touched only the blocks named in the findings.** Replace it with a diff check (changed blocks ⊆ blocks with findings) that the orchestrator can do itself. Evidence: 18 re-reviews → 0 new must_fix, 4 gloss-hygiene should_fix (§4); the largest fixes touched 10 blocks (ch001/s03, ch905/s02) and still produced nothing. Saves 2 subagent calls per round, i.e. 26 % of all reviewer runs on this corpus (18/68).
2. **Fold the meaning review into one reviewer for scenes ≤ ~400 words, and drop comprehension questions everywhere.** Keep one 'compare NL with EN: list omissions, additions, pronoun/addressee errors, fixed-phrase substitutions' section inside the language review. Evidence: meaning must_fix 2/25 scenes, both catchable by that list and one already duplicated by the language reviewer (§5); comprehension 34/34 answerable. For long dialogue/irony scenes (ch905-type tirades, ch907 Draco) a separate meaning pass can stay — those are where the meaning reviewer's should_fix clustered (voice 15).
3. **Make should_fix explicitly non-blocking, cap them, and batch the rest to the cohesion editor.** Evidence: 134 should_fix, ~40 % worth it by sample (§2.1), 0 % ever rejected in a round and 0 % applied outside a round (§2.2); one caused a revert (gewaden). Rule: reviewers may raise at most ~5 should_fix per scene and only of the kinds that scored: wrong-sense known word, dangling referent, un-Dutch collocation. Voice / over-explaining / gloss hygiene go to the chapter pass as notes.
4. **Turn the 'glossed hard word with a known equivalent' rule into an nl-check action.** For every `unknown`/`hard` + `glossed` row, look the gloss text up in the lexicon EN field; if a known lemma matches, print `replace-with: <lemma>`. Evidence: 6/22 must_fix were exactly this (schutting→hek, pocket→zachte kaft, gebonden→harde kaft, kaatste→antwoordde) and the tool had the row each time (§1.3).
5. **Add the polysemy watch-list and phrase list the reviewers asked for** (aankomen, vertrekken, gepast/passen, excuus, geest, hopen, binden; 'maar al te', 'hoe dan ook', 'zo nu en dan'). Evidence: 3 must_fix + ≥ 5 should_fix were sense-level misses of a lemma check (§1.3, §2.1); the `tool_feedback` of ch001/s01 already names the fix.
6. **Stop asking the reviewer to adjudicate `review`/`ignored` grammar flags; fix R-PARTICIPLE.** Only `likely` flags and L-FRAME go to the reviewer; the others become a one-line appendix. Suppress amod participles without complements and -ste superlatives. Evidence: 94 adjudications for 0 hits, 48 of them R-PARTICIPLE on the same three tokens (§3). Add an R-ER-PREP pattern for split 'er … prep' (parser miss that became a must_fix).
7. **Retune `nl-cohesion` C-TENSE** to ignore blocks that are dialogue, `\emph` thoughts, letters and epigraphs; keep C-TERM but check for the adjective form. Evidence: 13 flags, 0 confirmed (§6).
8. **Cohesion editor writes memory first, text second**: let it emit a findings list; only edits that change a *term* or *address* across scenes get a new version; gloss drops ride the next real edit. Evidence: 6 edits, 2 cosmetic, 1 a revert of round-1 work; 4 new versions + 8 tool runs for them (§6).
9. **Keep `max_fix_rounds = 2` as a ceiling but plan for one**: 9/9 rounds converged in one. Budget estimates should assume 1.0 rounds per flagged scene, and a flagged-scene rate of ~1/7 for V2 on experiment fragments, ~3/4 on the longer production scenes of ch001.

### Expected effect on cost per scene (reviewer subagent calls)

Today, a scene with one must_fix costs 2 reviews + 1 fix + 2 re-reviews = 5 role calls; a clean scene 2. With cuts 1–3: flagged scene = 1 (or 2 for long dialogue) review + 1 fix = 2–3; clean scene = 1–2. On this corpus: 68 reviewer runs → about 31 (one merged review per scene plus a separate meaning pass for the six ch905/ch907 dialogue scenes), with the only loss being the four gloss-hygiene should_fix that nl-check reports anyway and whatever a merged reviewer misses of the 2 meaning must_fix (both of which it is specifically instructed to look for).
